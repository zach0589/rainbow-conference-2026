Channel Thinking vs. System Thinking

Channel Thinking

  • "We need to be on LSA"
  • "Let's try running some Facebook ads"
  • "Our SEO guy handles that"
  • "We get leads, so it must be working"
  • Each channel operates independently

System Thinking

  • "Where does LSA fit in our overall strategy?"
  • "What happens after someone sees our ad?"
  • "How do all our channels support each other?"
  • "We track from first touch to closed job"
  • Every channel has a role in the full journey

What Is a Media Mix?

  • Your media mix is the combination of marketing channels you invest in
  • It defines where you show up, how you show up, and how often
  • A strong media mix balances three things:
    • Capturing demand that already exists
    • Creating demand before emergencies happen
    • Converting attention into booked jobs
  • No two markets require the exact same mix

The Three Layers of a Strong Media Mix

1. Demand Capture

Bottom of Funnel

  • Local Service Ads
  • Google Ads (PPC) on high-intent keywords
  • SEO for "near me" and emergency searches
  • Google Business Profile optimization

2. Demand Creation

Middle / Top of Funnel

  • Retargeting ads (display and social)
  • YouTube pre-roll ads
  • Social media content and paid social
  • Community involvement and branding

3. Trust & Conversion

The Revenue Layer

  • Review generation and management
  • Website speed and user experience
  • Speed to lead (call answering, response time)
  • Follow-up systems and CRM

The Conversion Layer: Where Revenue Is Won or Lost

  • Leads do not equal revenue
  • What happens between the click and the booked job matters more than the click itself
  • The conversion layer includes:
    • How fast you answer the phone
    • What your website looks like on mobile
    • Whether your reviews build trust or raise questions
    • How quickly you follow up on form submissions
  • Most companies lose 30-50% of their leads in this layer

The Most Common Mistakes We See

  • Over-relying on LSA as the only lead source
  • No retargeting -- letting website visitors leave and forget you
  • A website that looks outdated or loads slowly on mobile
  • Not answering calls within 3 rings or returning missed calls within 15 min
  • No system for tracking which channels actually produce revenue
  • Spending money without knowing your cost per booked job
  • Ignoring reviews or not actively generating them

What the Best Operators Do Differently

  • They invest across all three layers, not just demand capture
  • They track performance from first click to completed job
  • They treat their website and reviews as marketing assets, not afterthoughts
  • They answer the phone fast and follow up faster
  • They adjust their mix quarterly based on data, not gut feel
  • They think long-term (SEO, brand) while executing short-term (LSA, PPC)

The Marketing Funnel (Simplified)

1. Awareness

People learn you exist. They see your brand before they need you.

2. Consideration

They have a problem and are comparing options. Reading reviews, checking websites, asking around.

3. Decision

They're ready to call someone. Searching "water damage restoration near me" right now.

4. Conversion

They clicked, called, or submitted a form. Now they need to become a booked, completed job.

Why This Matters for Restoration

  • Restoration is emergency-driven: the decision stage is compressed
  • When someone needs you, they need you NOW
  • That means:
    • Trust must be built before the emergency
    • Speed is a competitive advantage at every stage
    • The company that shows up first (online and in person) usually wins
  • Your funnel has to account for both planned and panic decisions

Where Each Channel Fits in the Funnel

  • Community sponsorships and events

  • Vehicle wraps and yard signs

Consideration

  • Retargeting ads

Decision

  • Google Ads (PPC)

Conversion

  • Online reviews and reputation
  • Call answering and speed to lead
  • Follow-up systems and CRM

The Funnel Does Not End at the Click

  • Getting the lead is only half the job
  • The conversion layer is where revenue lives
  • Three questions to ask yourself:
    • What percentage of our leads become booked jobs?
    • How fast do we answer calls and return missed ones?
    • Would I hire us based on our website and reviews?
  • If you can't answer these, that's your first gap

Step 1: Map What You're Doing

  • Write down every marketing activity you're currently doing
  • For each one, answer two questions:
    • WHY are you doing this? (What's the goal?)
    • WHERE does it fit in the funnel? (Awareness, Consideration, Decision, or Conversion)
  • Don't overthink it. Just get it all on paper.

Step 2: Find the Gaps

  • Look at your funnel map
  • Which stages have the most activity? Which have none?
  • Common patterns we see:
    • Heavy on Decision (LSA, PPC) -- light on everything else
    • No Awareness activity at all
    • Nothing in Conversion beyond "we answer the phone"
  • Circle the biggest gap -- that's your #1 priority
